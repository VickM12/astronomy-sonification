Harmonia Mundi

An instrument made from orbits

This observatory shows the eight planets from Mercury through Neptune. Their places come from Astronomy Engine’s VSOP87 state vectors, rotated from the J2000 mean equator into the J2000 mean ecliptic. Distance and speed are the lengths of those vectors. Nothing in the view is a circular stand-in for a calculated position.

The time you set is UTC. Astronomy Engine then converts that instant to Terrestrial Time for the ephemeris. Sidereal periods, used for orbital frequencies, are the NASA NSSDCA values, not the tropical periods printed in the summary fact sheet.

Playback speed is simulated days per real-time second. Tempo chooses how long a crossing rings. The audio-seconds-per-day control spaces those notes on the audio clock. The three are separate. Seeking rebuilds the longitude anchors, so a jump does not fire the crossings it skipped, and the next step can detect those crossings again.

Press Play to hear the orbits. Scientific mode maps each sidereal frequency into sound, either by one shared scale factor or by folding each planet into a chosen octave. Musical mode starts from that same mapping and can snap the pitches to a scale. The formulas and the resulting hertz stay in the observation panel. Export to WAV or MIDI is still ahead.
